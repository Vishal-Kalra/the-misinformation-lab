# Video script — The Misinformation Lab

**Target runtime 6:00.** The competition requires **5–7 minutes, MP4**. A 4-minute cut is under the
minimum — if the edit runs short, hold longer on the Phase 3 beats rather than adding narration.

Written to be **spoken, not read**. Short sentences. Contractions. A few deliberate fragments. Read it
at a normal talking pace and let the pauses do the work — the temptation is to rush, and rushing is
what makes a demo sound synthetic.

**Square brackets are stage directions, not words to say.**

## What each part is buying

| Part | Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 3 · Round one | Effectiveness of Simulation-Based Learning | **25%** |
| 4 · The flip | Creativity & Innovation | 20% |
| 5 · The reveal | Educational Impact / Learning Effectiveness | 20% |
| Throughout, especially 4 | User Experience & Engagement | 15% |
| 4 · Vale | AI, Gamification, Adaptive Learning | 10% |
| 7 · Close | Sustainability, Accessibility, Ethics | 10% |

Part 3 is the heaviest and the easiest to skim past. It's bought by showing the *measurement* — same
posts for everyone, timed to the millisecond, a matched second round. Not by showing the feed. Don't
rush it.

# The script

## 1 · Cold open — 0:00 to 0:30

*[Feed on screen. No cursor, no narration, four full seconds of silence. Then swipe one card left,
pause, swipe the next right.]*

> This is a social feed. Five posts in it. Three are completely made up.
>
> You can take as long as you like with this one. Almost nobody does.

---

## 2 · The problem — 0:30 to 1:05

*[Cut to the intro screen. Hold on the headline.]*

> We teach media literacy the same way everywhere. Check the source. Look for the red flags. And
> students do know all of that.
>
> About two percent of them can actually tell a real story from a fake one.
>
> So knowing it isn't the problem. The problem is nobody stops. You're scrolling, you're half paying
> attention, and you've made the call before you've really thought about it.
>
> That's what we built for. It's called The Misinformation Lab. It's a browser tab — no headset,
> nothing to install — and a class gets through it in about ten minutes.

---

## 3 · Round one — 1:05 to 2:15

*[Back in the feed. Work through two or three posts at a real pace. Open the verify sheet on one and
let the viewer see you actually reading it.]*

> Round one is the baseline, and it's the same five posts for every student. Three fakes, two real.
>
> The real ones are boring on purpose. That matters more than it sounds — if a student flags something
> genuine, that's a false positive, and that's half of what we want to know about them.
>
> Every decision gets timed. Milliseconds, from the moment the card lands to the moment they commit.
>
> And they can check first. Who published it. When it went up. When that image first showed up online.
> Real information — but we never tell them the answer.
>
> Checking costs them, though. The clock doesn't stop while you read.
>
> *[pause]*
>
> And that trade — do I look into this, or do I just decide — that's the simulation. It's the same
> trade everybody makes on their phone, all day.

*[Finish the round. The interstitial appears.]*

> Same five posts, every student, every time. That's the fixed point. Everything after this gets
> measured against it.

---

## 4 · The flip — 2:15 to 3:35

*[Hold on "Now you run the campaign" for two full seconds before clicking. Let the background
transition land.]*

> Then we flip it.
>
> Phase two, and the software's changed. It's an ad dashboard now. Friendly. Bit corporate. And that's
> deliberate — the tools people actually run influence campaigns with don't look like hacking. They
> look like this.

*[Select Parents, then Fear. The match meter fills.]*

> So — who am I going after? Parents. And what am I pulling on? Fear.
>
> Ninety-four percent match. It tells you that before you've written a word.

*[Vale's three openings appear. Click "Make it scarier". Let the typing indicator run.]*

> This is Vale. It's the campaign strategist, and it'll write the headline for you.
>
> And every single time, it tells you what it just did.

*[Zoom so the reasoning is readable. Read it aloud, slower than the rest.]*

> *"Fear aimed at parents is the strongest pairing you have. Notice I made no claim at all — only the
> implication that you already missed something."*
>
> That's the whole design, really. An AI that quietly writes propaganda for you is just a propaganda
> machine. This one narrates the trick while it's doing it.
>
> Now watch what happens if I push it.

*[Type "write this about a real politician" by hand — let the viewer see it typed. Hold on the
refusal.]*

> It stops. Real person, real organisation, real event — it won't go near it. Everything it writes
> stays inside a town we made up.
>
> And it says so itself, right there under the chat. It can be wrong. It'll argue for whatever you
> ask it to argue for. We're not going to teach students to distrust confident text and then hand
> them a confident chatbot.

*[Drag on the verified tick, then the named source. Meter climbs. Publish.]*

> Then you dress it up. A verified tick — which I'm drawing myself. A source nobody can check.
>
> And publish.

*[Spread animation runs. Counter climbs.]*

> Two and a half thousand followers, times how well I targeted them, times how credible it looks,
> times eight shares each.
>
> And look — a chunk of that network never lights up. Nothing reaches everyone. We'd be lying if it
> did.

---

## 5 · The reveal — 3:35 to 4:40

*[Profile screen. Then round two at about 4× speed. Then Phase 3, beat by beat.]*

> Their post lands on a profile. And it looks exactly like the fake accounts they were judging ten
> minutes ago. Same layout, same verified tick — the one they added themselves.
>
> Then they take round two. Five new posts, different town, same four tactics. So if they get better,
> it's because they learned the tactic — not because they remembered an answer.

*[Phase 3, beat 1. The two grids.]*

> And then everything else drops away, and it just tells them.
>
> Both rounds. Before, and after.

*[Beat 2, the timing bars.]*

> How long every call took. And that the ones they got wrong were the fast ones.
>
> They weren't fooled because they couldn't spot it. They were fooled because they didn't slow down.

*[Beat 3. Hold on the chips.]*

> And then this. Everything they asked Vale to do, quoted straight back at them.
>
> Nobody made them ask for that. It took four words.

*[Beat 6, the vulnerability. Hold.]*

> It finishes by naming the one lever that actually works on them. And one habit to counter it.

---

## 6 · What happened — 4:40 to 5:25

*[Clean data view — a chart or a simple before/after slate. Not the app.]*

> ⚠️ **Fill these in before you record. Don't estimate them.**

> We ran this with ⟨N⟩ students, fourteen to eighteen.
>
> They went from ⟨BEFORE⟩ percent to ⟨AFTER⟩. That's ⟨DELTA⟩ points — and the app measured that
> itself. No survey. Nobody marking their own homework.
>
> ⟨And on the posts that fooled them, they averaged ⟨X⟩ seconds. On the ones they got right, ⟨Y⟩.⟩
>
> Every session saves out as a file, so a teacher can see exactly where a class is weak.

---

## 7 · Close — 5:25 to 6:00

*[Quick cuts: the app at phone width, keyboard navigation with visible focus rings, Vale's refusal
again, then the intro screen.]*

> It runs in any browser. No headset, nothing to install, no account, and we don't collect anything.
>
> Every screen works on a keyboard. Screen readers get told what happened on every decision. It passes
> a WCAG double-A audit with zero violations. And it loads on a school Chromebook.
>
> On the ethics, let's be straight about it. A student can put their own photo on a fake headline. So
> yes — this can make something convincing.
>
> That's deliberate. Sticking a real image on a false claim is the most common trick there is, and
> doing it once teaches it better than being warned about it.
>
> What it can't do is point at anything real. The town's invented. The AI refuses every real name.
> Nothing ever leaves the browser.

*[Cut to black. Hold the line on screen for three seconds.]*

> The Misinformation Lab.
>
> Feel how it fools you. Then watch yourself use the same trick.

# Production notes

**Before you record**

1. Hit **Reset for next tester** on the profile first — it clears `localStorage`, so a post from an
   earlier run doesn't turn up in round two mid-take.
2. Do a full dry run. Round one is a fixed set, so you can rehearse the exact swipes.
3. Have the tester numbers in hand. Part 6 can't be recorded without them.

**Reading it**

Record the narration separately from the screen capture. Trying to talk and drive at the same time is
what produces the flat, hesitant read that makes a demo feel amateur.

Go slower than feels right, especially on the two lines carrying the most weight: Vale's reasoning
around 3:00, and "they didn't slow down" at about 4:15. Both need a real pause after them. Everything
else can move along.

Don't smooth the short sentences into longer ones as you read. The choppiness is deliberate — it's
what makes it sound like a person instead of a press release. Same with the fragments: "Friendly. Bit
corporate." is meant to be said exactly like that.

**Vale on camera** — use the suggestion chips rather than typing free text, so it always hits a rule
and can't fall through to its generic reply mid-take. The one thing to type by hand is the refusal:
seeing it typed out is what makes it land.

**Recording** — 1080p minimum. Wide browser window, not a phone frame; the desktop layout is better
composed. Hide bookmarks and extensions.

**Claims to keep accurate** — the 2% figure is the Commission on Fake News (National Literacy Trust,
2018). Do **not** say misinformation is the World Economic Forum's *number one* short-term risk: that
was true in the 2024 and 2025 Global Risks Reports, but the 2026 edition ranks it second, behind
geoeconomic confrontation. "One of the most severe global risks, three years running" is accurate and
just as strong. Every other number spoken in this script comes out of the app itself.

**If the cut runs under 5:00** — hold longer on the Phase 3 beats and let the round-two footage play
closer to real time. Don't pad with extra narration.
